# 4. The chain of lifts and where 1/2 comes from

The exponent 1/2 is produced by `core_gm`, which bounds the Chebyshev-type function Gm(x) by x^{1/2+O(ε)} from a large bundle of hypotheses about a chain of m "lifts" with m ≈ √x. The reports trace 1/2 to a balance between a one-off cost of size x/m and a per-lift cost of size m, and trace the arithmetic content to a Bost-type slope inequality, a p-adic divisibility statement for the chain (proved by a Dwork-type analysis of theta kernels at supersingular CM points), and a congruence-module bound by Ш¹ lengths and twisted Selmer groups.

Status markers: [statement read], [proof outline read], [proof read in full], [inferred from names], [unread]. Paths are relative to `src/`.

## Terms used on this page

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hermitian lattice | a finitely generated projective 𝒪_K-module with a Hermitian metric at each infinite place (here K is a CM field) |
| slope μ, μ_max, μ_min | normalized Arakelov degree of a Hermitian lattice, and of its most and least positive sublattices |
| Bost's slope inequality | if a nonzero map φ : L → M has operator norm ≤ C at infinity, then μ_min(L) − log C ≤ μ_max(M); used to show a section cannot vanish to too high an order |
| congruence module | for a Hecke-stable subspace Z of a lattice of modular forms: (projection of the lattice onto Z) / (intersection of the lattice with Z). Its length measures congruences between Z and its complement (Ribet, Hida) |
| Ш¹(G_{E,S}, M) | classes in H¹ of the Galois group of E unramified outside S that are locally trivial at the places in S |
| ph(y) | y log y − y, a Stirling-type main term of log Γ(y + 1) |
| lift j | one step of the chain, j = 1, …, m. The reports read these as theta lifts between unitary groups [inferred from names] |

## Overview

`core_gm` is short and mostly bookkeeping: finite Fourier analysis on an abelian group H (the Galois group of a host field) followed by a real-variable size lemma. All arithmetic content sits in its hypothesis bundle `CoreAtH`, and inside that in `SizeData₂`, about eleven real inequalities about one record of numbers: slopes of Hermitian lattices, heights of maps, local "gains" at inert primes, a divisor term `DIV`, and counts. From these, `each_unit_bound_statement` derives

  ASK(u) ≤ Main(u) + ErrX(u),

where ASK is an explicit prime sum and Main is m copies of a Stirling main term, one per lift. Dividing by m and balancing the errors gives exponent 1/2. [proof outline read] (core-gm.md overview)

### The prime sum ASK and the main term

`DedekindLFunctions/RunBaseDimensionLeRankAddGenusTerm.lean:105,110`. [statement read] (core-gm.md Q2)

```lean
def ASK (τ) (a) (w) := (∑' p, if Inert p then 2 * log p * ∑' i, if Odd i then max (a / p^i - w (partner τ (frob p ^ i))) 0 else 0 else 0)
                     + log d * ∑' i, max (a / d^(i+1) - w (partner τ (gd (i+1)))) 0
def MAIN (a) (w) := a * (log a - 1 - γ + κ(prim ch).re - f⁻¹ * ∑ ρ ∈ Phi, log (w ρ))
```

ASK runs over primes p inert in ℚ(√±d), with weight 2 log p on odd powers, counting (a/p^i − w)⁺, where the threshold w depends on the Frobenius class of p^i in H. For inert p, 2 log p·1_{i odd} = Λ(p^i)(1 − χ_d(p^i)), the von Mangoldt function of ζ(s)/L(s, χ_d) [inferred from names] (frontier-a-divisor.md Q3). Without w this is the shape of Legendre's formula log(a!) = Σ_p log p Σ_i ⌊a/p^i⌋, and MAIN is its Stirling term [inferred from names] (core-gm.md Q2).

`Deduction.ch_balance` (`…/LandauTheorems/ChBalance.lean:200`) proves an identity ASK − MAIN = (Fourier combination of G-functions), using (a/n − w)⁺ = w·(y/n − 1)⁺ with y = a/w. So the Legendre shape is chosen so that it is exactly the Riesz-smoothed sum G at y = a/w ≈ x. [statement read], first lines of proof read (frontier-a-divisor.md Q3); the rest of `ch_balance` is [unread] (core-gm.md Q6).

## The divisor inequality (`DIV`)

[proof read in full] for the arithmetic lemma; see below for the inputs (frontier-a-divisor.md Q1).

```lean
muC + hu - 2 * Fo ≤ ∑ t ∈ TT, (m * (ph (aT t) - aT t * logOm) - aT t * logT)
```

### The surrounding slope argument

`DivClauses` (`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/DivisionHeightBoundStatement.lean:553`), established by `divClauses_of` [proof read in full], has four clauses for each word u (frontier-a-divisor.md Q1c, Q2):
1. **Integrality**: a map B_u sends the lattice Nu, twisted by a finite-place divisor sg (the gains), into the dual lattice RR^∨. This comes from a field `val2` of `DivInputs`: a period pairing has w-adic valuation ≥ sg(w).
2. **Archimedean bound**: B_u factors as a chain of m matrices K_0, …, K_{m−1}; its height is at most the sum of the step heights (`height_chain`, [proof outline read]).
3. **`DIV`**, as above.
4. **Slope of the twisted lattice**: μ(shiftLat Nu sg) = μ(Nu) + 2Σ_w lw(w)·⌊sg w⌋.

`Formalism.HN.SlopeIneq` (`Formalism/HarderNarasimhan/Statement.lean:91`) is literally Bost's slope inequality [statement read]. The classical reading is the Arakelov slope method: "a nonzero section vanishing to order sg cannot have deg(sg) exceeding the degree" [inferred from names] (frontier-a-divisor.md Q1c). It is not a Siegel-lemma construction and not Hilbert–Samuel.

## Why 1/2: the x/m + m balance

After `core` divides by |TT|·m·b·(δ2/2), the error per unit has three parts ([statement read] for `SizeWindows`, [proof outline read] for `proposition_size_bound`, `Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/ChainSlopeWindows/LipschitzWeightAndMinimum.lean:507`; core-gm.md Q3 item 5):

| Term | Size | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| nT·aE·(1 + m²/k)/(nT·m·b) | ≈ x/m | one-off losses of size x·b per t (the a·La and a·log T terms, the final pairing) |
| m·(2n·m·b·x^ε)/(nT·m·b) | ≈ m·x^{O(ε)} | per-lift losses B0 = f·m·b·x^ε, accumulated over m lifts |
| nT2·k·b·x^{2ε}/(n²·nT) | ≈ k/n² ≤ x^{1/2+O(ε)} | rank term |

With x^{1/2−ε} ≤ m ≤ x^{1/2+ε/2}, each term is ≤ x^{1/2+13ε/8}, and the size lemma concludes Gx ≤ x^{1/2+(C+3)ε}. **Square-root cancellation comes from balancing x/m against m.** The signal grows linearly in the number of lifts; each lift's cost also grows linearly in m, so the total cost grows quadratically. (core-gm.md Q3; frontier-a-divisor.md Q5 confirms)

Why each lift costs ∝ m: the Hodge–Tate shape of each lift, `ShapeJumps k m t` (`EmbeddingTypes.lean:99`), has exactly m "small" jumps, and `JumpConditions` requires jW ≤ deg(u)·m. Each jump costs about b/ℓ + log_ℓ b of local lattice index. That gives B0 ∝ m per lift. [statement read] for the table entries; the Fontaine–Laffaille / Wach reading is [inferred from names] (frontier-b-links.md Q5)

There is no induction or bootstrap on m, the level or the exponent in `core_gm`, `ch_core`, `ch_top`, `each_unit_bound_statement`, `proposition_size_bound`, `ChainTied` or `topDesWin`: all are straight-line. The only induction is the structural `ChainTie.chain_le`, which composes the chain once. No pigeonhole, Siegel-lemma or Stepanov-type auxiliary construction was seen. [proof outline read] (core-gm.md Q3, Q4) The later frontier-f-divisibility.md found one more induction of the same kind, `main_ind`, which composes the m local steps and adds their gains; it improves no bound iteratively either (Task 3).

## How Ш¹ enters each lift (`LinkN`)

```lean

  etaSum L j ≤ dZ0 j * (C * (f * m * b * L^2 + log x ^ 2 * Nc) + ∑' l, vvTerm D l)
```

`HypV` states the window bound `VVle` for the family; `divinN_of_hypV` (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/DivinNOfHypV.lean:117`, [proof read in full]) combines it with `LinkN`. Since `VWin` supplies √b and only b is needed, **the Ш term is lower order**.

**What etaJ is.** etaJ(j, w) is the length of a **congruence module**: the projection of the integral lattice of the j-th lift's forms onto Hecke eigenspaces Zgood, modulo its intersection with them (`Divisibility/LiftLat.lean:116-147`, `RowLat.lean:153-155`; Galois-side twin `NData.etaLoc`, `LevelLetterCocycles/DataN.lean:219`). `TwistedPlaces.adm1` bounds it by Σ multiplicity × (congruence number vcc(π) + v_ℓ|level group|).
